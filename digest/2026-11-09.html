Daily Research Digest: Nov 9, 2026

Source caveat: Search was thin and mostly returned aggregators and roundups, not primary reporting. Items marked (reported) are secondhand and should be verified before anyone acts on them. Window is about 48 hours, plus some late-last-week items because the last 24 hours were quiet.

Top 3 things to know

  1. Humanoid money is far ahead of humanoid work. Dealroom puts humanoid funding at about $11B through early October, more than double all of 2025. Counterpoint's H1 data says over 60% of shipments went to entertainment, data production and research. Capital is pricing in deployment that mostly hasn't happened yet.
  2. Heavy-equipment autonomy is where the real money is going. Bedrock Robotics raised a $600M Series C at $4.1B post-money, with CapitalG and Emergence co-leading. It sells autonomy kits that retrofit excavators and other heavy equipment. That is a narrow, deployable wedge with clear unit economics, unlike general-purpose humanoids.
  3. Frontier-lab valuations are wobbling at the top. OpenAI has reportedly raised $11B so far at a $1.25T valuation, short of the earlier $30B at $1.4T target, and its IPO is now expected in 2027. Bloomberg also reported that Anthropic IPO buyers may value it as low as $1.1T, versus about $2T expected a month ago. Read this as late-cycle price discovery, not a collapse.

Robotics & physical AI

  • Bedrock Robotics, $600M Series C: The strongest signal of the week (see above). Autonomy for construction and mining is a real field-deployment story.
  • Foundry Robotics, $100M Series A, and Machenta, $40M seed: Machenta says its robot model learns from one demonstration. That claim is unproven. Treat it as hype until there is a demo with a success rate on unseen tasks.
  • Tutor Intelligence, $75M Series B led by a16z (reported): The company builds robot foundation models. Check whether it has paying warehouse or logistics customers before getting excited.
  • MIT's WorldParticle: One learned "physical world model" simulates deformable objects, granular materials and proteins. It beat numerical methods on accuracy in the authors' tests and is about 100x faster on protein dynamics (lead author's claim). Relevant to sim-for-robotics and soft manipulation. The paper is arXiv 2605.15305, going to SIGGRAPH Asia. It's a research result, not a product, and I haven't seen independent replication.
  • Consumer humanoid hype check: Tuesday Labs' Voxel home robot is priced at about $20K with a $200 reservation deposit, but the hardware shown is a prototype that will be redesigned. Unitree's stunt videos don't disclose how much was autonomous. Average humanoid price is about $29.6K (IDC estimate), down from about $37K a year ago.
  • Agility Robotics: Search results indicate its roughly $2.5B SPAC was approved by shareholders and it is listing publicly. I couldn't open the article, so verify the details. If true, it's a rare public-market read on a deployed humanoid business.

AI/ML research & models

  • Quiet 24 hours for releases. The BenchLM tracker showed none in the last day and six in the past week.
  • Recent releases: OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber (Nov 5), Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling 2 Small (Nov 5), Cognition's SWE-2 Flash (Nov 4) and Tencent's Hy4 (Nov 4). The tracker gives no benchmark scores, so I can't say which of these matter. I'd wait for independent evals.
  • Research worth tracking: WorldParticle is the one clear result I found. Learned general-purpose simulators are the trend to watch for physical AI.
  • Policy: Sen. Cantwell reportedly released a six-principle AI framework with enforceable NIST safety standards, pre-release audits and incident reporting. This is a framework, not legislation, and I'd rate its near-term impact as low.

VC & startup deals

  • Thinking Machines Lab (reported): Closing about $5B at a $40B pre-money valuation, led by Accel (Business Insider via a secondhand roundup).
  • FuriosaAI, $620M Series D (about $2.9B post-money): AI inference chips. Inference silicon is getting funded as a way to avoid Nvidia dependence.
  • Week of Oct 26 to Nov 1: Primary AI funding was $2.43B across 16 rounds of $20M or more. Dulo, a hardware-design AI startup, raised a $130M seed.
  • M&A: Guidewire is buying hyperexponential for $1.7B cash plus up to $400M earnout. Itaú Unibanco is buying NeoSpace for about $589M.
  • Concentration: Roughly 2.5% of deals took about 65% of venture dollars in 2025 (CRV, via a roundup). Valuations are extreme, and sub-$100M rounds remain the more rational part of the market.
  • Unverified: A digest lists large humanoid and agent rounds (XDOF $1.2B Series B, Figure $675M, 1X $125M Series B). It gives no dates or sourcing, so I'm not relying on them.

Worth a closer look

Bedrock Robotics and the "retrofit autonomy for heavy equipment" category. It has a defined customer, a hardware-light deployment model and a big TAM in labor-constrained construction. A $4.1B valuation is rich, so the more interesting angle for a seed or Series A investor is the adjacent layer. That includes teleop and remote-assist tooling, site-mapping and perception data, and autonomy for other off-road verticals such as mining, agriculture and ports. On the research side, I'd read the WorldParticle paper for its relevance to learned simulators for manipulation.
