Daily Research Digest: Nov 7, 2026

Caveat: search coverage of the last 24 hours was thin, especially in robotics. I widened to roughly the past week. Items are dated where I could confirm a date, and I marked them where I couldn't.

Top 3 things to know

  1. Open-weight models keep closing on the frontier. Moonshot released the full Kimi K3.1 Pro weights (about 2.8T total and 104B active parameters, modified MIT license). A benchmark aggregator ranks it 12th overall, its highest open-weight placement. For deployment, this lowers the cost floor for anyone building on top of models.
  2. Money is still concentrating in a few huge rounds. October global venture funding was about $59B, up 45% year over year. DeepSeek's reported $15.9B raise at about $90B led it. Seven billion-dollar-plus rounds made up just over 40% of the total, and AI took about 66%. Headline growth is mostly mega-rounds, not breadth.
  3. Physical AI is shifting from model demos to capital-heavy operations. Bedrock Robotics (autonomy for construction equipment) raised $600M at a $4.1B post-money valuation. Foundry Robotics raised $100M to own and run robot-staffed factories. Investors are now funding the operating model, not just the robot.

Robotics & physical AI

  • Bedrock Robotics: $600M Series C at $4.1B post-money (CapitalG and Emergence co-led, week of Oct 26). Takeaway: autonomy retrofitted onto existing heavy equipment is attracting real money. It is a more deployable wedge than general humanoids.
  • Foundry Robotics: $100M Series A (Marlinspike led; Khosla and others participated; Oct 28). It is a manufacturing-as-a-service model: it owns the facility, deploys robotic cells and runs production for customers (drones, satellites and similar). Takeaway: this is a bet on utilization and unit economics, not on selling robots. It is interesting, but execution risk is high.
  • Agility Robotics going public via SPAC at about $2.5B (reported with about $300M in Digit orders). Takeaway: this is the first real public-market test of humanoid pricing. Coverage points to a discount to private peers, so watch the multiple. Exact timing was not confirmed in my sources.
  • AgiBot rolled off its 15,000th robot. Takeaway: Chinese humanoid makers are ahead on unit volume. Counts are company-reported and say nothing about task reliability or revenue per robot.
  • XPENG's physical AI unit raised over $900M at a $6.3B valuation (Aug 24, so older context). XPENG targets mass production of its IRON humanoid by end of 2026, with customer deliveries in 2027. Hype check: initial deployment is in its own stores and campuses.
  • Hype watch: NEURA's "up to $1.4B" Series C (June) and its "millions of robots by 2030" target are company-stated, and "up to" does real work in that headline.

AI/ML research & models

  • Kimi K3.1 Pro weights released (Moonshot, listed as of Nov 2). API pricing is $2/$10 per million tokens. The license carries conditions for very large commercial users.
  • OpenAI GPT-6 Cyber (Nov 5) is gated to vetted customers and aimed at vulnerability and exploit work. The ExploitGym score of 48.5% vs 42.4% for GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI-reported, so treat it as a vendor claim.
  • Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling 2 Small (Nov 5) is a mixture-of-experts model (594B total, 17B active, 1M context) with Apache 2.0 weights promised after safety testing. Watch whether the weights actually ship.
  • Smaller releases: Ricursive's Ripple-65B Preview (Apache 2.0, Nov 4; the claimed $32M training cost is unverified), Cognition's SWE-2 Flash, and Tencent's Hy4 (weights not yet public).
  • Benchmark honesty check: four new benchmarks landed this week, and no lab led both its own and the cross-lab versions. Discount lab-run leaderboards. One arXiv audit of physical-AI benchmark redundancy is also worth a skim if you evaluate robot-learning claims.

VC & startup deals

  • October totals: $59B globally, with the US at $28B and China at $20.6B. Moonshot AI reportedly reached a $50B valuation and filed confidentially for a Hong Kong IPO. Anthropic's IPO was expected in November.
  • Other large October rounds: Re:Build $2.1B, Axiom $2B, Ramp $1.85B, Tenstorrent $1.5B, Kalshi $1B.
  • Last week's AI total: $2.43B across 16 rounds of $20M or more. FuriosaAI raised a $620M Series D, with roughly $280M from Korea's National Growth Fund. Serval raised $150M at $3B.
  • M&A: Guidewire is buying hyperexponential for $1.7B cash plus up to $400M in earnout. Uber agreed to buy ezCater for $2.3B. At least nine $1B-plus acquisitions of venture-backed companies closed in October.
  • Small robotics rounds: URobotics ($30M Series A, autonomous mobility, Nov 6) and Anvil Robotics ($5.5M seed, modular robot-building platform). I could not read the URobotics page, so the details are from the search snippet only.

Worth a closer look

Foundry Robotics (manufacturing-as-a-service). The claim is that robotic cells can make complex electro-mechanical products (defense and space hardware) at lower cost with faster iteration than contract manufacturers. Diligence questions: cell changeover time between products, real cost per unit against a human line, customer concentration in defense, and whether owning the buildings turns this into a capital-intensive real-estate business. Bedrock Robotics is the companion comparison: a software-and-retrofit model that reached a $4.1B valuation. It is worth checking whether the market is pricing hardware-light autonomy above owned-asset operators.
