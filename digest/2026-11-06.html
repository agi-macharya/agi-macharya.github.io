Daily Research Digest: Nov 6, 2026

Search coverage was thin on model and paper news. The trackers listed names but few benchmark numbers. I could not verify some items against primary sources, and I flag those below.

Top 3 things to know

  1. AI equities sold off hard on Nov 5. The Nasdaq fell about 5.5% into correction territory, and the S&P fell about 4.4%. Nvidia dropped about 9% and AMD about 17%. The stated causes are doubts that AI infrastructure spending will earn its keep, debt worries, and political backlash against data centers after the midterms. This does not show demand collapsing, but expect private marks and late-stage pricing to feel it over the next few weeks.
  2. Agility Robotics is now the first pure-play humanoid public company. It merged with a SPAC (Churchill Capital XI) at about a $2.5B equity value and expects about $465M gross proceeds. The valuation is far ahead of the fundamentals: revenue is tiny against heavy losses (see the robotics section).
  3. Physical-AI and defense-autonomy money is still flowing despite the selloff. Zipline raised $1.5B at a $21.5B post-money valuation. Shield AI is buying radar maker Echodyne for about $5.3B and raising about $2.5B across equity, structured equity and debt. These are real-asset, deployed-product businesses, which is what capital is rewarding.

Robotics & physical AI

  • Agility (AGLT): Digit is deployed with nine named-class customers (Schaeffler, GXO, Toyota Canada and Mercado Libre among them), mostly moving totes. It has no complex manipulation. Takeaway: treat this as a public-market sentiment test for humanoids, not proof of unit economics.
  • Agility financials conflict across sources. TechCrunch cites H1 2026 revenue of about $174K and a net loss of about $362M. Another roundup cites 2025 revenue of about $1.8M and a net loss of about $138M. The periods differ, so read the S-4 or 10-Q before relying on either. Either way, revenue is a rounding error against the loss.
  • Agility pipeline is unproven: about $300M of planned contracts from 30 unnamed customers for Digit 5, with a CES 2027 demo and wide availability around end of 2027. Takeaway: the whole story rests on Digit 5 uptime and accuracy, which nobody has seen.
  • Zipline: the $1.5B round was led by Paradigm, WCM and Layer Global. It has a new Walmart deal for about 300 more locations and plans US manufacturing expansion. Takeaway: this is the clearest example of an autonomy company with real operational volume.
  • Bedrock Robotics (reported last week): $600M Series C at about $4.1B post-money. It sells autonomy kits that retrofit excavators and other heavy equipment. Takeaway: retrofit autonomy in constrained industrial settings is working better than general-purpose humanoids.
  • Embodied AI "Air" (Switzerland): a wheeled humanoid with compliant tendon-driven arms, shown at Humanoid Europe in Munich. The robot learns from teleoperation and reportedly goes autonomous in about a month. Its lead investor is Faber, with no amount disclosed. Takeaway: the 98% reliability figure comes from one evaluation and is company-reported. Treat it as a pitch, not a result.
  • Other small rounds: Laza Medical (robotic cath-lab imaging, up to $160M structured, GE HealthCare-led), Foundry Robotics ($100M Series A) and Machenta ($40M seed, simulation-trained robot model).

AI/ML research & models

  • OpenAI GPT-6 Cyber (Nov 5): a restricted vulnerability-research model released through its Daybreak Red program. It scored 48.5% on ExploitGym against 32.1% for the prior cyber model. It also reportedly found 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox flaws. Takeaway: these are vendor-reported figures with no independent replication yet. If they hold, offensive-security automation just took a real step.
  • Other releases Nov 4–5, by name only: Cognition's SWE-2 Flash, Tencent's Hy4, Ricursive Intelligence's Ripple-65B Preview and Celeris-1.1 Decision. I found no benchmark numbers, so I can't say whether any matter.
  • Sand.ai (Beijing): raised $200M for its generative video work. Its MAGI-2 is an autoregressive (not diffusion) video model of roughly 114B parameters with about 6B active. The product's reported $10M+ ARR is company-reported. Takeaway: the autoregressive approach is worth watching for world-model and simulation use.
  • Nvidia and China: reportedly about 600K H200 chips are sitting in Hong Kong while Beijing withholds large-scale import approval. Nvidia has not confirmed it.

VC & startup deals

  • Shield AI / Echodyne: about $5.3B acquisition, plus a $1B Series H at a $28.8B post-money valuation. Blackstone is putting in $1B of structured equity and JPMorgan is leading $500M of debt. The deal pairs radar with Hivemind autonomy software and is expected to close in early 2027. Takeaway: defense autonomy is where the late-stage capital is concentrated.
  • AMD acquisitions (unverified, from a summary only): reportedly World Labs (about $8.2B in stock) and Taalas (about $2.9B). Confirm in AMD's filing before using it. If true, it is a significant signal for spatial or world-model companies.
  • Microsoft and the neoclouds: reportedly tightening oversight of Nscale, Nebius and IREN. Those stocks fell 13% or more. Takeaway: the AI-infrastructure financing chain is where stress will show first.
  • Funding last week: about $2.43B across 16 rounds of $20M+. The largest were FuriosaAI's $620M Series D, Bedrock's $600M and Serval's $150M.
  • Other AI rounds this week: Sentra ($150M, AI data security), Pliant ($80M, payments for AI agents) and Ocient ($100M, data platform for agents).
  • Wonder (Marc Lore): confidentially filed for an IPO. It owns Spyce, a kitchen-robotics company.

Worth a closer look

Retrofit autonomy for heavy equipment and logistics, in Bedrock's mold. Bedrock's valuation has repriced fast, but a selloff in public AI could reopen entry points for seed and A-stage teams in adjacent verticals (mining, ports, agriculture). The sector needs deployed hours and customer retention, not demos. Agility's public filings will also give the first audited look at humanoid deployment economics. Digit's real throughput and uptime numbers would be a useful benchmark for any humanoid pitch you see.

Caveats

  • Several figures come from aggregator roundups, not primary filings. The Agility numbers conflict.
  • The AMD deal and some roundup details rest on summaries I could not confirm.
  • I found no notable robotics research papers in this window.
