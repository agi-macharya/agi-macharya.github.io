Daily Research Digest: Nov 10, 2026

Most of this comes from secondary aggregators and press-release feeds. Primary sources were not checked.

Top 3 things to know

  1. Shield AI agreed to buy radar maker Echodyne for $5.3B (announced Nov 5). Bloomberg reportedly puts Shield AI's value at about $28.8B. It is the biggest deep-tech deal in the window and shows defense autonomy companies buying sensing hardware to own the full stack.
  2. RoboColosseum, a blind head-to-head test of robot policies across 124 sites (5,699 trials), found no single winner. No policy completed any of the five Unitree G1 humanoid tasks. It is the most useful reality check of the week, and NVIDIA's GR00T 2 claims should be read against it.
  3. Robotics is the second-largest AI funding sector this month by dollars. One tracker counts about $1.7B across 6 rounds, behind foundation models at $5.1B. Zipline's $1.5B round makes up most of it.

Robotics & physical AI

  • NVIDIA GR00T 2 and Isaac Lab 3.0 were announced at CoRL. GR00T 2 is a 2B-parameter vision-language-action model that reportedly averages 71% one-shot success across 50 tasks. Weights and dataset are due in December. The 71% figure comes from a summary page, so wait for independent evals.
  • Zipline raised $1.5B at a $21.5B valuation (Nov 6). A drone delivery company with its own aircraft, launch systems and logistics software, and one of the few physical-AI businesses with real operating scale.
  • UC Berkeley and NVIDIA presented "agentic robotics," where coding agents write and verify robot code instead of relying on large motion datasets. Interesting if it holds up outside simulation.
  • Boston Dynamics posted a video of Atlas on wheels, testing model-based and whole-body RL control. A demo, not a product signal.
  • Deployments are mostly incremental. ANYbotics began delivering ANYmal X, Intel and SAP showed an autonomous warehouse demo, and ID Logistics and Terra Robotics plan a humanoid trial with no site or robot chosen yet. Treat the trial as hype until a site is named.
  • Market share claim: AGIBOT says it holds about 43% of H1 global humanoid shipments (citing Counterpoint), with about 2B yuan in revenue. This is a company claim.
  • Smaller items: Serve Robotics launched "Physical AI Services" (connectivity, data curation, sidewalk-derived datasets), a data-monetization pivot. Nature Will announced a 50B-parameter model for dexterous hands with no independent results seen. Robbyant released LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model spanning three sensor types.

AI/ML research & models

  • Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 (Nov 9), and Meta released Muse Ember the same day. BenchLM puts Fable 5.2 first at 91.2 (an estimate), then Gemini 4 Carbon at 88.1 and Claude Opus 5.5 at 85.8. Gaps this size rarely predict real deployment differences.
  • GPT-6.1 Sol scores 85.0 on BenchLM, about 93% of the top score at an output price BenchLM says is roughly 75% lower. For cost-sensitive products the price gap matters more.
  • Best open-weight model is Kimi K3.1 Pro (about 2.8T total parameters, 104B active) at 74.6, a clear gap to the closed frontier.
  • FrontierPhysics (56 research-level physics tasks, 22 models): every model scored under 30%. Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code led at 27.4%, then GPT-6 Astra at 23.2%.
  • Route Anything: perfect per-input routing across models beats the best single model by about 5.4 points, but learned routers capture only a small share of that.
  • VFlash (NVIDIA): a small helper model for VLM robot planning, claiming about 80% lower latency on Jetson Thor. Task-success gains (70.2% vs 62.6%) are mostly from simulation or recorded data.
  • LoCo cuts reasoning length by 42-45% on math tests, but was tested on one 7B model only.

The paper roundup used covers Nov 1-7, so Nov 8-10 papers are thin.

VC & startup deals

  • OpenAI's round is open at $11B so far at a $1.25T valuation, with SoftBank and MGX named. It could reach about $20B, below the earlier $30B target and the $1.5T valuation first sought. A soft signal at the top of the market.
  • Thinking Machines Lab announced a $5.0B seed at about a $40B valuation (Nov 6, lead undisclosed).
  • Cyera raised $800M at a $20B valuation (AI data security), and Vanta agreed to acquire Pillar Security (terms undisclosed). AI security is consolidating quickly.
  • Recent robotics rounds just before the window: Foundry Robotics $100M Series A (Oct 31), Tutor Intelligence $75M (Nov 3, robot workers rented by the hour), MMI $125M (Nov 1, robotic microsurgery).
  • Seed-stage: Rogue Cortex (UK) $4.5M for robotics development tooling, EDGE Sound Research $2M for physical-AI perception.
  • Quiet days: one tracker reported no new AI round above $2M first announced on Nov 7-8.

Worth a closer look

Robot evaluation and data infrastructure. RoboColosseum shows nobody can credibly claim a general robot policy today. NVIDIA is open-sourcing models and simulation, Serve is selling its data, and Nebius/Positronic and Honda are running manipulation competitions. The gap is trusted, real-world, cross-site evaluation and the data layer around it. A startup doing third-party robot benchmarking, or deployment-grounded datasets that foundation-model teams pay for, looks more defensible than another policy model. Tutor Intelligence's per-hour robot pricing is a test of whether robots-as-a-service works at scale.
