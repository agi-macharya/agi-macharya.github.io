Daily Research Digest: Nov 11, 2026

Source note: Most of this comes from secondary coverage and press releases. One of the two funding articles (Mercor) is an AI-written rewrite of the company's own announcement. Robotics headlines come from a Robot Report homepage snapshot, and I did not open the linked stories. Model releases on Nov 9–11 come from a single tracker, so treat them as reported and unverified. I could not read one daily-news digest, so coverage is thinner than I wanted.

Top 3 things to know

  1. Path Robotics raised $300M at a $2.45B valuation. The Series E was led by Georgian, with coverage dated Nov 10. The bet is that welding data transfers to other factory tasks. That transfer is the unproven part.
  2. Axon put $1.2B into Skydio and became its US public-safety distributor. This is dated Nov 9, from a headline only. It ties a drone maker to a large channel.
  3. NVIDIA launched GR00T 2 and Isaac Lab 3.0 at CoRL. This is dated Nov 9, from a headline only. Check how it is licensed and what hardware it targets before taking it seriously.

Robotics & physical AI

  • Serve Robotics opened its sidewalk fleet through a public API (Nov 11). The fleet is about 2,000 robots across 20 US cities. Live access needs Serve's approval and there is a simulator sandbox. Whether outside developers show up is the open question. Serve reported 2026 revenue guidance of about $12M, so the scale is still small.
  • Boston Dynamics put wheels on Atlas to test whole-body control (Nov 9, headline only). Read it as a research testbed. It says nothing yet about a product.
  • China reportedly blocked SoftBank's $5.4B ABB Robotics deal. This is from a headline only. Cross-border robotics M&A now carries real regulatory risk.
  • North American robot orders rose 24.8% in Q3, per the A3 industry group. The figure is reported by A3 and I did not check it. It is a decent demand signal for industrial automation.
  • Agility Robotics began trading on Nasdaq. This is from a headline only. It could set a public-market reference for humanoid valuations, but I have no price or first-day data.

AI/ML research & models

  • Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 are listed for Nov 9. This is reported by one tracker with no benchmark numbers. I haven't verified it.
  • Meta's Muse Ember (Nov 9) and Baidu's ERNIE 5.5 Flash Preview (Nov 11) are also listed. Same caveat: single tracker, no numbers.
  • The Nov 5 open-weights and coding-model claims are all lab-reported. Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro and Cognition's SWE-2 Flash fall here. One tracker notes Moonshot's own table shows a large gap on another benchmark, so discount the headline numbers.
  • MIT CSAIL's weight-space programming work shows a big MATH-500 jump, from 7.9% to 48.1%. An independent rebuild reached 51.0%. The tracker says much of the gain comes from the base model looping, so the method may matter less than it looks.

VC & startup deals

  • Mercor raised $750M at a $20B valuation (Nov 10). The Series D included an employee tender, with NVIDIA, Salesforce, Benchmark and others participating. Valuation and "5 million experts" are company claims.
  • Path Robotics: $300M Series E at $2.45B. Bloomberg's headline rounds this to $2.5B, which is a rounding difference only.
  • Axon's $1.2B into Skydio is the largest robotics check in this batch (headline only).
  • Earlier robotics rounds are still circulating but are not new. Humanoid's $152M Series A is from July. I did not confirm the dates on the other rounds that came up.

Worth a closer look

Serve Robotics' developer API. Opening a deployed physical fleet to third parties through an API is a distribution play. It lets app builders get robot delivery without owning hardware. For deal evaluation, the signals to watch are the number of approved developers, delivery volume per robot, and whether revenue beyond Serve's own deliveries appears. Everything here comes from Serve's own press release, so none of it is independently verified.
